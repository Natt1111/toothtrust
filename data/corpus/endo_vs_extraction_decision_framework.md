## Summary

The decision to retain a tooth via root canal treatment (RCT) or extract and replace it is one of the most impactful treatment planning decisions in dentistry. Both options have evolved substantially: implant osseointegration success rates have prompted extraction as a default in some practices, while evidence-based endodontic outcomes argue for tooth retention in many clinical scenarios. The AAE advocates for an evidence-driven, patient-centered framework that weighs restorability, periodontal prognosis, endodontic prognosis, strategic value, systemic factors, and patient preferences.

Neither option is inherently superior; the literature supports comparable long-term outcomes for well-selected cases in either pathway. However, preserving natural teeth — with their proprioceptive feedback, natural bone preservation, and cost-effectiveness — should be the default when feasible.

## Clinical Indications

### Factors Favoring Endodontic Treatment (Tooth Retention)
- **Favorable endodontic prognosis**: Vital or infected pulp in a tooth with sufficient root length, adequate canal anatomy, and no VRF; expected success rate >85% with proper technique.
- **Adequate restorability**: Sufficient coronal structure for a crown (ideally ≥1.5–2 mm ferrule); crown lengthening or orthodontic extrusion can improve prognosis if structure is deficient.
- **Adequate periodontal support**: Bone levels and attachment sufficient to support the restored tooth under function.
- **Strategic importance**: Abutment for a bridge; last molar for chewing function; key anchor for removable prosthesis.
- **Patient preference for tooth retention**: Informed patient who understands the prognosis and values natural dentition.
- **Cost consideration**: In many clinical contexts, RCT + crown is comparable in long-term cost to implant placement + crown, but avoids the staged surgical process.
- **Systemic risk factors for implant**: Bisphosphonate use, uncontrolled diabetes, radiation to the jaw, anticoagulant therapy, and immunosuppression increase implant failure risk and may tip the balance toward tooth retention.

### Factors Favoring Extraction
- **Vertical root fracture**: Non-salvageable; extraction is definitive.
- **Severe periodontal disease**: Bone loss to or beyond the apex; advanced furcation involvement in multi-rooted teeth; attachment levels incompatible with function.
- **Non-restorable crown**: Caries or fracture extends below the crestal bone with insufficient structure for crown lengthening.
- **Severely resorbed roots**: Root resorption compromising structural integrity.
- **Hopeless endodontic anatomy**: Severe calcification, separated instrument in critical location, or external root resorption precluding adequate treatment.
- **Strategic non-value**: A tooth with no adjacent teeth to support a bridge and contralateral implant space; extraction with implant may be superior.
- **Cost constraints**: When financial barriers make multi-step RCT + crown unfeasible and implant costs are similarly prohibitive, extraction with partial denture may be the pragmatic path.

---

## Contraindications

- **RCT on a tooth with VRF**: No endodontic treatment can address a vertical root fracture.
- **RCT when crown is non-restorable without establishing ferrule**: Performing RCT before confirming restorability wastes resources; assess restorability before committing to endodontic treatment.
- **Immediate implant in a site with active infection**: Extraction must be followed by resolution of acute infection before implant placement.

## Evidence Notes

- **Outcomes parity**: Hannahan & Eleazer (2008, *J Endod*) found no statistically significant difference in 10-year survival between implants and endodontically treated teeth. Setzer & Kim (2014) meta-analysis confirmed similar long-term survival rates (92–97% for implants; 90–95% for RCT + crown).
- **Proprioception advantage**: Natural teeth have periodontal ligament mechanoreceptors providing tactile feedback; implants lack this. Functional studies show superior chewing efficiency and force modulation in natural teeth.
- **Bone preservation**: The PDL of a retained tooth maintains alveolar bone through functional stimulation; extraction triggers bone resorption regardless of implant placement timing.
- **Retreatment option**: Failed RCT allows retreatment or apicoectomy before extraction; a failed implant has fewer rescue options.
- **Systemic correlations**: Endodontic treatment does not meaningfully increase systemic bacteremia risk beyond what daily activities (toothbrushing) produce, per AAE position statement.
- **CBCT guidance**: CBCT significantly improves pre-treatment assessment of anatomy, bone loss, and fracture — improving the accuracy of the endo vs. extraction decision (Patel et al., 2012).

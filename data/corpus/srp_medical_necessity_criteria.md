## Summary

Scaling and root planing (SRP), also known as periodontal debridement or non-surgical periodontal therapy (NSPT), is the cornerstone of active periodontal disease treatment. SRP involves mechanical removal of supra- and subgingival plaque, calculus, and contaminated cementum to promote tissue healing and halt disease progression. Proper documentation of medical necessity is critical for insurance reimbursement (CDT D4341/D4342) and for defensible clinical records.

The AAP emphasizes that SRP is indicated based on clinical findings, not by a fixed probing depth number alone. A diagnosis of periodontitis — established through clinical attachment level measurement, radiographic bone loss, and staging per the 2017 AAP classification — is required to justify SRP.

---

## Clinical Indications

### Criteria for Medical Necessity

**Primary Clinical Indicators:**
- Clinical attachment loss (CAL) ≥1–2 mm beyond normal anatomic expectations.
- Radiographic evidence of alveolar bone loss.
- Probing depths ≥4–5 mm with bleeding on probing (BOP), indicating active disease.
- Subgingival calculus deposits confirmed by probing and radiographic assessment.

**2017 AAP Classification Thresholds:**
- SRP is appropriate for AAP Stage I–III periodontitis.
- Stage IV may require additional surgical planning concurrent with or following SRP.
- SRP in a patient with gingivitis (no CAL, no bone loss) is generally not covered and not indicated as periodontal treatment; prophylaxis (D1110) is appropriate for gingivitis.

**Documentation Requirements for SRP (Insurance Standard):**
1. **Periodontal chart**: Full 6-point probing (mesio-buccal, buccal, disto-buccal, mesio-lingual, lingual, disto-lingual) with depths, BOP, recession, furcation involvement.
2. **Radiographic series**: Current full-mouth radiographs or bitewing series demonstrating bone levels; radiographs must be diagnostic quality (not outdated by >36 months for active disease).
3. **Diagnosis**: Documented periodontal diagnosis using 2017 AAP classification (Stage/Grade).
4. **Treatment narrative**: Brief note explaining clinical findings, indicating areas of calculus, pocket depths, and disease activity.
5. **Medical history update**: Relevant systemic conditions (diabetes, cardiovascular disease, smoking) documented as risk modifiers affecting grade.

### CDT Code Selection
- **D4341** — Periodontal scaling and root planing, four or more teeth per quadrant.
- **D4342** — Periodontal scaling and root planing, one to three teeth per quadrant (localized SRP).
- **D4355** — Full-mouth debridement — used when periodontal examination cannot be completed due to heavy generalized calculus; precedes comprehensive periodontal evaluation.
- **D4910** — Periodontal maintenance (post-SRP recall for previously treated periodontitis patients; NOT a substitute for active therapy).

## Contraindications

- **SRP in a non-periodontitis patient**: SRP is not indicated for gingivitis (no CAL, no bone loss); prophylaxis or full-mouth debridement is appropriate.
- **SRP without current radiographs**: Cannot confirm bone loss or detect subgingival calculus extent without radiographs; documentation is incomplete.
- **SRP in a bleeding disorder without hematologic consultation**: Significant subgingival instrumentation induces bleeding; patients on anticoagulants (warfarin, NOACs) or with hemophilia require medical consultation.
- **Acute necrotizing periodontal disease**: SRP in the acute phase of NUG/NUP is deferred until the acute infection is controlled with local debridement, chlorhexidine, and antibiotics.
- **Areas with periodontal abscess**: Acute periodontal abscess requires drainage and antibiotics; SRP in the acute site is performed after resolution.

## Evidence Notes

- **Efficacy**: A Cochrane review (Van der Weijden & Timmerman, 2002; updated literature) confirms SRP reduces probing depths by 0.5–1.5 mm and gains 0.3–0.6 mm of clinical attachment in moderate periodontitis; effect is greater in deeper pockets (>6 mm).
- **Microbiological effect**: SRP reduces the total subgingival microbial load and shifts the composition from gram-negative anaerobes toward a healthier gram-positive profile; effects persist for 3–6 months with sustained oral hygiene.
- **Systemic effects**: Meta-analyses (Sanz et al., 2020, *J Clin Periodontol*) demonstrate that treating periodontitis reduces HbA1c by ~0.4% in diabetic patients, systemic CRP by ~0.5 mg/L, and is associated with reduced cardiovascular event risk.
- **SRP vs. surgery**: Badersten et al. (1984, classic study) and subsequent RCTs show that SRP and surgical therapy produce similar attachment gain at pockets ≤6 mm; surgery shows advantage at pockets >6 mm.
- **Adjunct antibiotics**: Systemic metronidazole + amoxicillin (Haffajee protocol) shows additional benefit for Stage III–IV periodontitis when added to SRP, particularly in young aggressive forms; local antibiotics (Arestin, Atridox) show modest additional benefit in deep pockets.

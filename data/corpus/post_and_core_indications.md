## Summary

A post and core system is used to restore endodontically treated teeth with insufficient coronal tooth structure to retain a definitive crown. The post is placed within the root canal space to provide retention for the core buildup, which reconstructs the coronal form. The system does not strengthen the tooth root — a common misconception — but rather transfers load to the remaining root structure. Material selection (cast metal post-core, prefabricated metal post with composite core, fiber post with composite core) depends on the root morphology, remaining tooth structure, and loading demands.

The decision to place a post must be preceded by confirmation of adequate endodontic obturation, sufficient ferrule (≥1.5–2 mm of circumferential dentin above the crown margin), and acceptable root length and width for post placement without weakening the root.

---

## Clinical Indications

### When a Post Is Indicated
- **Insufficient coronal dentin** for core retention: when remaining coronal structure cannot provide adequate retention and resistance form for a crown without supplemental post support.
- **Single-rooted, heavily restored teeth**: Maxillary and mandibular central/lateral incisors, canines, and premolars with large access preparations and few remaining coronal walls.
- **Functionally loaded anterior teeth**: High esthetic and functional demands require a stable core foundation.

### When a Post Is NOT Indicated
- **Adequate ferrule and coronal structure present**: If sufficient sound dentin circumferentially (ferrule) and core buildup height can be achieved without a post, omitting the post preserves root structure.
- **Short or narrow roots**: Post placement in roots with insufficient length (<8–9 mm post length achievable) or thin root walls risks root perforation and fracture.
- **Molars**: Molars have multiple broad roots; the pulp chamber alone typically provides adequate retention for a core buildup without posts. When needed, shorter posts in the largest canal are preferred.

### Post Type Selection
| Post Type | Indications | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|-----------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| Prefabricated fiber post (FRP) | Anterior/premolar; esthetic zone; moderate load | Tooth-colored; modulus near dentin; retrievable | Lower retention than cast; core separation risk |
| Prefabricated metal post | Posterior; adequate canal size | Cost-effective; strong | Stress concentration; corrosion |
| Cast metal post-core | Severely compromised, divergent or irregular canals | Custom fit; single-piece | Two appointments; expensive; rigid (fracture risk) |

### Ferrule Effect
The ferrule — a circumferential band of sound dentin (≥1.5–2 mm height, ≥1 mm thick) — is the single most important factor in restored tooth survival. Without adequate ferrule:
- Crown fracture rates increase substantially.
- Post placement alone cannot compensate for lack of ferrule.
- Crown lengthening surgery or orthodontic extrusion may be required to establish ferrule before restoration.

### Core Materials
- **Composite resin core**: Standard; bonds to fiber post; adequate strength.
- **Amalgam core**: Durable but declining in use; no adhesive bond; requires mechanical retention from post and remaining walls.
- **Glass ionomer**: Low strength; reserved for limited-load situations.
- **Dual-cure resin cement**: Required for fiber post cementation to ensure cure at apical extent of post.

## Contraindications

- **Active periapical pathology**: Post placement must wait until endodontic success is confirmed (typically 6–12 months for resolution of periapical lesion, or at time of obturation for cases without pathology).
- **Inadequate root length**: Minimum 8–9 mm of post length required in the root; remaining apical seal (3–5 mm of gutta-percha) must be maintained.
- **Root fractures or resorption**: Compromised root integrity precludes post placement.
- **Non-restorable prognosis**: If ferrule and bone support are inadequate despite surgical options, extraction is preferred over complex post-crown reconstruction.
- **Teeth with poor periodontal prognosis**: Investing in post-core-crown on a periodontally compromised tooth is not cost-effective.

## Evidence Notes

- **Ferrule research**: Tan et al. (2005, *Int Endod J*) meta-analysis confirmed the ferrule as the dominant predictor of endodontically treated tooth survival; teeth without ferrule had 3× higher failure risk.
- **Fiber post survival**: A systematic review (Marchionatti et al., 2017) found 5-year survival of fiber post-restored teeth at 92–96%; no significant difference from metal posts when adequate ferrule was present.
- **Post necessity**: Naumann et al. (2012) RCT showed no significant difference in crown survival between fiber-post and no-post restorations in premolars with >50% remaining coronal structure at 5 years, supporting selective post use.
- **Root fracture risk**: Cast metal post-cores are associated with catastrophic root fracture (non-retrievable failure) more frequently than fiber posts, which fail through debonding (retrievable).
- **Post length**: At least half the root length (or as long as the crown height) should be engaged by the post for adequate retention, without violating the 3–5 mm apical seal.

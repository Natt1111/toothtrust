## Summary

Radiographic assessment of alveolar bone levels is an essential component of periodontal diagnosis, staging, and treatment monitoring. Conventional intraoral radiographs (bitewings, periapicals) and cone-beam CT (CBCT) provide complementary information about bone support, defect morphology, and disease progression. Accurate interpretation requires understanding of normal bone anatomy, radiographic technique principles, and the limitations of 2D imaging in capturing 3D bone architecture.

Radiographic bone loss is a key criterion in AAP 2017 Stage/Grade assignment and insurance documentation for SRP. The radiograph must be current (typically within 24 months for maintenance patients; shorter intervals with active disease) and of diagnostic quality.

## Clinical Indications

### Normal Alveolar Bone Landmarks
- **Alveolar crest position**: Normally 1–2 mm apical to the cementoenamel junction (CEJ).
- **Lamina dura**: Thin radiopaque line at the alveolar crest and along the root surface; loss or discontinuity suggests inflammation or bone resorption.
- **Trabecular bone pattern**: Variable; coarser in mandibular posterior, finer in maxillary anterior.
- **PDL space**: Thin radiolucent line (0.15–0.38 mm) around the root; widening suggests increased function, inflammation, or resorption.

### Bone Loss Patterns

**Horizontal Bone Loss**
- Uniform reduction in alveolar bone height perpendicular to the root surface.
- Most common pattern in chronic generalized periodontitis.
- Characterized by: crest >3 mm apical to CEJ on radiograph (threshold varies; CEJ-bone distance >2 mm is often cited as abnormal).
- Defect type: suprabony pockets with horizontal bone loss are generally associated with less favorable prognosis for regeneration.

**Vertical (Angular) Bone Loss**
- Non-uniform bone loss creating an angular defect alongside the root surface.
- Associated with intrabony (infrabony) pockets; the defect base is apical to the alveolar crest.
- Classified by the number of remaining bony walls:
  - 3-wall defect: most favorable for regeneration (GTR, bone graft).
  - 2-wall: intermediate.
  - 1-wall (hemiseptal): least favorable.
- Best visualized on periapical radiographs; vertical bitewings improve detection over standard horizontal bitewings.

**Furcation Involvement**
- Class I–III furcation involvement may be suggested radiographically by "furcation arrow" — a radiolucency at the furcation entrance.
- Radiographic furcation involvement underestimates clinical involvement; probing is required to classify furcation severity.

**Bone Loss Quantification for AAP Staging**
- Stage I: Bone loss in coronal 1/3 (<15% of root length).
- Stage II: Bone loss in coronal 1/3 (15–33%).
- Stage III/IV: Bone loss extends to middle or apical 1/3 of root (>33%).
- Percentage bone loss = (root length − remaining bone support) ÷ root length × 100.

## Contraindications

- **Using outdated radiographs for bone level assessment**: Radiographs >24–36 months old may not reflect current bone levels in an active patient; current images are required.
- **Relying on radiographs alone without clinical probing**: Radiographs detect bone loss at ≥30–40% mineral content loss; early or thin-plate bone loss may be invisible radiographically. Clinical probing is required for complete assessment.
- **Using panoramic radiograph as primary periodontal assessment tool**: Panoramic radiographs are useful screening tools but have significant geometric distortion; periapical or bitewing radiographs provide more accurate bone level measurement.

---

## Evidence Notes

- **Radiographic sensitivity**: Benn (1990) and Gröndahl (2002) studies confirm that conventional radiographs miss early bone loss (<30–40% mineral) and underestimate bone defect depth and morphology.
- **Bitewing angulation**: Steep horizontal angulation (cone-cut error) or incorrect vertical angulation introduces magnification and foreshortening errors; paralleling technique with proper beam alignment is required.
- **CBCT for bone defects**: CBCT provides 3D defect morphology, furcation classification, and buccal/lingual bone levels invisible on 2D films; AAOMR recommends CBCT for presurgical planning when 2D films are inadequate (Tyndall & Brooks, 2000).
- **Crestal lamina dura loss**: Disappearance of the cribriform plate radiographically is associated with active bone resorption; this finding should prompt clinical probing to assess activity.
- **Periapical vs. bitewing**: Periapical films provide better visualization of the entire root and apex; bitewing films are superior for detecting early interproximal bone loss due to reduced foreshortening.

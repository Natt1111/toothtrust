## Summary

Meta-analyses and systematic reviews form the highest level of evidence for endodontic treatment outcomes. This document synthesizes key findings from landmark systematic reviews on root canal treatment (RCT) success and survival rates, prognostic factors, and comparisons with alternative treatments. The body of evidence supports RCT as a durable, evidence-based intervention with outcomes comparable to single-tooth implants when appropriate selection criteria are applied.

The distinction between "success" (complete radiographic healing + symptom absence) and "survival" (tooth still present and functional) is critical in interpreting the literature. Most administrative dataset studies report survival; histopathological or strict radiographic studies report success.

---

## Clinical Indications

### Summary of Key Meta-Analyses

**Ng et al. (2008, 2011) — Most Comprehensive RCT Outcome Review**
- Systematic review of 63 studies for Part 1 (technical factors) and additional studies for Part 2 (clinical factors).
- Strict success rate (complete healing): 68–85% across studies.
- Weighted success: ~74% for teeth with preoperative periapical lesions; ~92% for vital teeth without periapical lesions.
- Key prognostic predictors: adequate root filling length, adequate canal taper, absence of preoperative periapical pathology, adequate coronal restoration.

**Torabinejad et al. (2009) — Retreatment vs. Surgery**
- Non-surgical retreatment: 77–83% success.
- Periradicular surgery: 77–94% success.
- Combined approach (orthograde + surgical): highest overall success.
- Conclusion: Retreatment and surgery are complementary; case selection drives outcome.

**Salehrabi & Rotstein (2004) — Administrative Database**
- 1,462,936 teeth treated by endodontists; 97% retention at 8 years.
- Molar retention slightly lower than anterior/premolar.
- This represents the largest single dataset in endodontic literature.

**Iqbal & Kim (2007) — RCT vs. Implant**
- No statistically significant difference in long-term survival between endodontically treated teeth and single implants.
- Methodological limitations (heterogeneous studies) acknowledged.
- Supports case-by-case decision rather than systematic preference for either.

**Azarpazhooh & Leake (2006) — Systemic Effects**
- Inadequate evidence to support association between RCT and systemic disease (challenged the "focal infection" theory revival).
- AAE position: properly performed RCT does not increase systemic bacteremia risk.

### Prognostic Factor Summary Table

| Factor | Direction of Effect on Success |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| Preoperative periapical lesion | Negative (74% vs. 92%) |
| Obturation length 0–2mm from apex | Positive |
| Obturation length >2mm short | Strongly negative |
| Coronal restoration quality | Positive (strong effect) |
| Operator (endodontist vs. GP) | Positive for endodontist |
| Tooth type (anterior vs. molar) | Anterior slightly higher |
| Patient diabetes (uncontrolled) | Negative |
| Missed canals | Strongly negative (↑ failure) |

## Contraindications

- **Extrapolating population averages to individual cases**: Meta-analytic findings apply at the group level; individual prognosis must incorporate patient-specific factors.
- **Treating with RCT based solely on outcome statistics**: Indication for RCT remains clinical (pulpal/periapical diagnosis), not statistical.

---

## Evidence Notes

- **Publication bias**: The endodontic outcomes literature has well-documented publication bias favoring positive outcomes; systematic reviews with rigorous methodology (Ng, 2008) attempt to correct for this but note persistent risk.
- **Heterogeneity**: Definitions of "success" vary substantially across studies (radiographic criteria, time points, symptom thresholds), making direct comparison difficult.
- **CBCT-based outcomes**: CBCT-based healing assessment identifies more residual AP than conventional radiography, suggesting that conventional "success" rates may overestimate true healing. Patel et al. (2019) found ~15% discordance between conventional and CBCT outcomes at 12 months.
- **Quality of obturation vs. coronal seal**: An influential review (Tronstad et al., 2000) found that quality of coronal restoration was at least as important as obturation quality in determining long-term success — reinforcing the importance of timely crown placement.
- **Evidence grade**: The majority of endodontic outcome evidence is Level III (retrospective cohort) or Level IV (case series); randomized controlled trials are uncommon due to ethical constraints in dentistry.

## Summary

CAMBRA (Caries Management by Risk Assessment) is a clinical protocol developed by the California Dental Association and UCSF that operationalizes evidence-based caries management through systematic risk stratification. Rather than treating caries solely as a mechanical problem (drill and fill), CAMBRA frames it as an infectious, transmissible disease with modifiable biological risk and protective factors. The protocol assigns patients to low, moderate, high, or extreme risk categories and maps each category to specific preventive and restorative interventions.

CAMBRA forms are available for age-specific cohorts (0–5 years and 6 years through adult) and include standardized data collection for disease indicators, risk factors, and protective factors. The protocol is intended to be reassessed at each recall interval.

## Clinical Indications

### Risk Stratification Framework

**Disease Indicators (Shift toward High/Extreme Risk)**
- Visible cavitated or non-cavitated (white spot) lesions
- Radiographic evidence of proximal caries
- Restorations placed in the last 3 years (indicates active caries history)

**Risk Factors (Biological and Behavioral)**
- *High*: Mutans streptococci (MS) levels ≥10⁵ CFU/mL (CariScreen or culture)
- Moderate-to-high sugar exposure frequency (≥3×/day between meals)
- Inadequate saliva flow (unstimulated <0.1 mL/min; stimulated <0.7 mL/min)
- Xerostomia-inducing medications (antihistamines, antidepressants, diuretics)
- Deep pits and fissures; exposed root surfaces
- Orthodontic appliances or removable partial dentures
- Recreational drug use (methamphetamine — "meth mouth")
- Eating disorders (bulimia — acid erosion increases caries risk)
- Low socioeconomic status / limited dental access

**Protective Factors (Shift toward Lower Risk)**
- Fluoride exposure: community water fluoridation, fluoride toothpaste, professional fluoride application
- Antibacterial use: chlorhexidine 0.12% rinse (10-day/month protocol in high-risk patients)
- Adequate saliva flow; saliva-stimulating agents (xylitol, pilocarpine)
- Regular recall intervals with professional care
- Dental sealants on at-risk pits and fissures

### Intervention Protocols by Risk Level

| Risk Level | Recall | Radiographs | Fluoride | CHX | Sealants |
|------------|--------|-------------|---------|-----|---------|
| Low | 12 months | Per ADA guidelines | OTC fluoride toothpaste | Not indicated | As appropriate |
| Moderate | 6 months | Bitewing 12–24 months | 5,000 ppm Rx fluoride | Consider | Indicated |
| High | 3–4 months | Bitewing 6 months | 5,000 ppm + tray | 10-day/month CHX | Indicated |
| Extreme | 3 months | Bitewing 6 months | 5,000 ppm + MI Paste | CHX + consider iodine | Indicated |

## Contraindications

- **CHX contraindicated** in patients with known hypersensitivity to chlorhexidine; use with caution in patients with composite restorations (potential staining at margins).
- **High-dose fluoride (5,000 ppm) in children**: Prescription fluoride toothpaste for children under 6 requires careful monitoring to prevent fluoride ingestion; topical gels at this concentration require tray delivery with suction.
- **CAMBRA does not replace radiographic diagnosis**: Biological risk stratification guides prevention but radiographs remain essential for caries detection and staging.

## Evidence Notes

- **Validation**: Tellez et al. (2013, *JADA*) validated CAMBRA forms against clinical caries incidence and found significant correlation between risk category and caries increment over 4 years.
- **Salivary testing**: CariScreen and GC Saliva-Check meter have demonstrated moderate-to-good correlation with culture-based MS levels in clinical validation studies (Oho et al., 2000).
- **Fluoride efficacy**: A Cochrane review (Walsh et al., 2010) confirmed that professionally applied fluoride varnish reduces caries incidence by 43% in permanent teeth; fluoride toothpaste at ≥1,000 ppm is strongly evidence-supported.
- **CHX efficacy debate**: While short-course CHX reduces MS counts, a Cochrane review (James et al., 2010) found limited evidence that CHX alone reduces caries incidence in permanent teeth; it is most effective as adjunct to fluoride in high-risk patients.
- **CAMBRA adoption**: As of 2019, over 40 US dental schools teach CAMBRA or CAMBRA-equivalent caries management curricula; EHR integration is variable.

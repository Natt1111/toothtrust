## Summary

Minimally Invasive Dentistry (MID) is a philosophy and clinical framework that prioritizes preservation of sound tooth structure, remineralization of early lesions, and the use of the smallest effective restorative intervention. Formalized by the FDI World Dental Federation and the Mount Holan Consensus, MID challenges the historical "extension for prevention" paradigm of G.V. Black by asserting that the best cavity preparation is the least destructive one compatible with the available materials and clinical goals.

MID integrates caries risk assessment (see CAMBRA), modern adhesive materials, and micro-invasive techniques (sealants, resin infiltration, ART) to defer or minimize surgical intervention. It is increasingly the standard of care in evidence-based dental curricula worldwide.

---

## Clinical Indications

### Remineralization (Non-Operative Management)
- **ICDAS 1–2 lesions**: Early enamel demineralization without cavitation is amenable to remineralization via fluoride, casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate (CPP-ACP/MI Paste), and dietary modification.
- **White spot lesions post-orthodontics**: Resin infiltration (Icon, DMG) is indicated for non-cavitated proximal and labial white spots.
- **Active proximal lesions (radiographic enamel only)**: Sealant placement over the pits and fissures combined with proximal resin infiltration can arrest progression.

### Micro-Invasive Techniques
- **Pit and fissure sealants**: Indicated for caries-susceptible pits/fissures in newly erupted molars and premolars; resin-based sealants with 50–80% 5-year retention rates reduce occlusal caries by ~70%.
- **Atraumatic Restorative Treatment (ART)**: Hand excavation and glass ionomer restoration; indicated in resource-limited settings or anxious patients; suitable for single-surface lesions in primary or permanent teeth.
- **Air abrasion**: Kinetic cavity preparation with aluminum oxide or bioactive glass particles; removes minimal tooth structure; indicated for early lesions and sealant surface preparation.

### Operative MID Principles
- **Selective caries removal**: The modern standard is selective (not complete) caries removal — hard, leathery dentin at the pulpal floor is retained under a liner/base when close to the pulp (see: pulp capping). Over-excavation risks iatrogenic pulp exposure.
- **Tunnel preparation**: Proximal Class II without enamel removal; viable when the marginal ridge is intact.
- **Sono-abrasion**: Ultrasonic tips for micro-cavity preparation; removes caries selectively with minimal collateral damage.

### Materials Supporting MID
- **Glass ionomer cement (GIC)**: Chemically bonds to dentin; releases fluoride; suitable for high-risk patients, primary teeth, and ART; lower mechanical strength than composite.
- **Resin-modified GIC (RMGIC)**: Improved strength and aesthetics; bridges performance gap.
- **Giomer and bioactive composites**: Incorporate fluoride-releasing components; suitable for MID-compatible restorations.

## Contraindications

- **Cavitated lesions (ICDAS ≥ 4–5)**: Remineralization cannot close a cavitated defect; operative intervention is required.
- **Pulpal involvement suspected**: MID techniques are not appropriate when there is deep caries with pulp proximity, irreversible pulpitis, or necrosis.
- **Sealant over cavitated lesion**: Placing a sealant over a frank cavity does not arrest the lesion; diagnosis must confirm non-cavitation before sealing.
- **ART in stress-bearing posterior sites**: ART glass ionomer is not durable in multi-surface, high-load preparations in permanent posterior teeth; composite or indirect restoration is preferred.

## Evidence Notes

- **Selective caries removal**: A Cochrane review (Ricketts et al., 2013) found no significant difference in pulp exposure rates or restoration failure between stepwise excavation and complete removal; guidelines have since shifted toward single-step selective removal.
- **Sealant efficacy**: Ahovuo-Saloranta et al. (2013, Cochrane) confirmed that resin-based pit and fissure sealants reduce occlusal caries by 11–51% at 5 years compared to no sealant.
- **Resin infiltration**: Paris et al. (2013) demonstrated stabilization of proximal ICDAS 2 lesions over 3 years with Icon resin infiltration vs. monitoring alone.
- **Biological vs. surgical balance**: Mount & Hume (1998) proposed the "Minimal Intervention in Dentistry" concept. Frencken et al. (2012, *J Dent Res*) defined the evidence base for the FDI MID policy.
- **Economic argument**: A health economic analysis (Fontana et al., 2020, *JADA*) found that integrating MID protocols and risk assessment reduces restorative costs by delaying or preventing operative intervention in moderate-risk patients.

## Summary

Bitewing radiographs are the most frequently used intraoral radiographic examination in general dentistry. They are primarily indicated for the detection of interproximal caries, assessment of alveolar crestal bone levels, and monitoring of existing restorations. A full bitewing series typically consists of two (posterior premolar + molar) or four images (separate premolar and molar BWs bilaterally), obtained with a film or digital sensor and beam-indicating device to achieve consistent geometry.

Accurate interpretation requires systematic examination of each anatomic structure in a defined sequence to avoid errors of omission. The ADA and AAOMR publish evidence-based guidelines for radiographic selection criteria, recommending BW intervals based on patient age, caries risk, and disease history.

## Clinical Indications

### ADA/AAOMR Bitewing Frequency Guidelines

| Patient Category | Recommended BW Interval |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| Child (primary teeth, no proximal contacts) | None until contacts closed |
| Child (primary, contacts closed, low risk) | 12–24 months |
| Child (primary, contacts closed, high risk) | 6–12 months |
| Adolescent (permanent, low risk) | 18–36 months |
| Adolescent (permanent, high risk) | 6–12 months |
| Adult (low risk, no caries, good OH) | 24–36 months |
| Adult (moderate risk) | 12–18 months |
| Adult (high risk: active caries, xerostomia, multiple restorations) | 6–12 months |

### Systematic Interpretation Sequence

**Step 1 — Image Quality Assessment**
- Correct exposure (adequate density/contrast — not over/underexposed).
- No cone-cutting or overlapping of proximal contacts.
- Bitewing tab placement: crowns and 2–3 mm of alveolar crest visible on both arches.
- Correct vertical angulation: no excessive foreshortening or elongation.

**Step 2 — Interproximal Caries Assessment**
Evaluate each proximal contact systematically (posterior right → posterior left, maxillary then mandibular):
- **Initial enamel lesion (E1)**: Faint radiolucency outer half of enamel; manage with remineralization.
- **Enamel lesion involving inner half (E2)**: Radiolucency inner enamel; consider sealing.
- **Dentin caries (D1)**: Radiolucency involving outer 1/3 dentin without pulp proximity; operative indicated.
- **Deep dentin caries (D2)**: Radiolucency involving inner 2/3 dentin with pulp proximity; IPC or pulp therapy consideration.

**Step 3 — Occlusal and Buccal/Lingual Surface Assessment**
- Radiolucent areas beneath occlusal surfaces (secondary caries under restorations, recurrent caries).
- Secondary caries at existing restoration margins: check for radiolucent gap at margin.

**Step 4 — Existing Restorations**
- Marginal integrity (gap, overhang, ditching).
- Amalgam: homogeneous density, no cracks or fractures.
- Composite: may be radiolucent (difficult to distinguish from recurrent caries without clinical correlation).
- Crowns: margin adaptation, recurrent caries at margin.

**Step 5 — Alveolar Crest**
- Normal: 1–2 mm apical to CEJ; crisp lamina dura at crest.
- Horizontal bone loss: crest >3 mm apical to CEJ.
- Vertical defects: angular bone loss adjacent to a root.
- Furcation involvement clue: "furcal arrow" radiolucency at molar furcations.

**Step 6 — Pulp Chambers**
- Calcified pulps: reduced pulp chamber size (dentin sclerosis from age or caries stimulus).
- Pulp stones: irregular calcifications within chamber.

**Step 7 — Root Tips and Crestal Bone**
- Residual root tips in edentulous spaces.
- Bony pathology in crestal area.

## Contraindications

- **BWs as substitute for periapical radiographs for endodontic evaluation**: BWs do not show the apical region; periapical films are required for endodontic diagnosis.
- **Routine BWs at intervals shorter than ADA guidelines without clinical indication**: Increases radiation exposure without proportionate diagnostic gain.
- **Pregnancy**: Dental radiographs are safe during pregnancy with proper lead apron shielding; unnecessary radiographs should be deferred to post-partum, but diagnostically needed radiographs should not be withheld.

## Evidence Notes

- **Radiation dose**: Modern digital BW receptors require ~50–80% less radiation than conventional film; dose per BW image is 1–5 microsieverts (µSv), compared to 0.1–0.3 mSv annual background radiation.
- **Caries detection sensitivity**: Bitewing radiographs have sensitivity of ~40–70% for proximal caries (higher for dentin lesions, lower for enamel-only); ICDAS clinical examination is complementary.
- **Digital vs. film**: Digital systems (direct and phosphor plate) offer comparable diagnostic accuracy to F-speed film with lower dose; image manipulation (brightness/contrast) improves lesion detection by ~10–15%.
- **AI-assisted interpretation**: Multiple FDA-cleared AI tools (Overjet, Pearl, VideaHealth) demonstrate radiographic caries detection AUC of 0.87–0.92 in validation studies, comparable to general dentist performance.

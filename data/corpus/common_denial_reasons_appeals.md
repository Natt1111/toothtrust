## Summary

Claim denials are a major source of revenue loss, administrative burden, and patient confusion in dental practices. Understanding the most common denial reasons — and the appropriate documentation and appeal strategies for each — is essential for efficient practice management and protecting patient access to care. The ADA estimates that a significant percentage of denied claims are overturned on appeal, meaning that well-crafted appeals with proper documentation are a high-value activity.

Appeals must be submitted within the timeframe specified in the plan's Explanation of Benefits (EOB) — typically 30–180 days from the denial date.

---

## Clinical Indications

### Common Denial Reasons and Responses

**1. "Procedure Not Covered / Benefit Not Available"**
- Plan simply does not cover the procedure.
- Response: Confirm coverage with patient's benefit summary. If not covered, patient is responsible; provide estimate before treatment.
- Note: Non-covered does not mean not necessary — document for patient record.

**2. "Missing or Incomplete Documentation"**
- Claim submitted without required radiographs, narrative, or supporting records.
- Response: Resubmit with complete documentation (current radiographs, periodontal chart, clinical narrative). Use payer's specific submission format.

**3. "Frequency Limitation"**
- Procedure performed within the plan's frequency restriction (e.g., crown within 5 years, BWs within 12 months).
- Response:
  - Provide documentation of exceptional circumstances: fracture, secondary caries, catastrophic failure of prior restoration.
  - Include clinical photographs, pre- and post-failure radiographs.
  - Submit clinical narrative explaining why early replacement was medically necessary.

**4. "Not Medically Necessary / Alternative Treatment Available"**
- Plan's consultant believes a less expensive treatment was appropriate.
- Response:
  - Document clinical necessity using evidence-based criteria (e.g., tooth structure loss >50% intercuspal width for crown; periapical pathology for RCT).
  - Cite clinical guidelines (ADA, AAP, AAE) in the appeal narrative.
  - Request a clinical peer review by a licensed dentist reviewer (patients have this right in most states).

**5. "Missing Tooth Exclusion"**
- Plan excludes replacement of teeth missing before coverage began.
- Response: Provide prior radiographs or records showing the tooth was present when coverage started. If tooth was extracted during coverage period, document the date and reason.

**6. "Waiting Period Not Satisfied"**
- Procedure performed before the plan's waiting period elapsed (common for major services in new plans).
- Response: Verify enrollment and waiting period dates. If correctly denied, document for patient financial planning purposes.

**7. "Downgrade / Alternative Benefit"**
- Payer pays for a lower-cost alternative (e.g., pays for PFM when all-ceramic was placed; pays for composite when posterior composite billed).
- Response: This is a plan contract issue, not a denial of coverage. Patient pays the difference; no appeal needed unless payer misapplied the contract terms.

**8. "Radiograph Not Acceptable Quality or Currency"**
- Submitted radiograph is outdated, overexposed, or does not show the area of interest.
- Response: Submit new diagnostic-quality images. Document clinical rationale for why imaging was obtained at that time.

**9. "Bundling / Global Package"**
- Payer bundles a separately billable procedure into another (e.g., bundles core buildup into crown fee).
- Response: Appeal with documentation that the core buildup required separate instrumentation, materials, and clinical time distinct from crown preparation. Cite CDT definitions.

**10. "Pre-Authorization Not Obtained"**
- High-cost procedure (crown, implant, periodontal surgery) performed without required pre-auth.
- Response: Limited appeal options; work to recover partial payment. Prevention: Establish pre-auth workflows for procedures over plan thresholds.

## Contraindications

- **Appealing without new information**: Sending the same claim a second time without additional documentation rarely succeeds; appeals must contain new evidence or a rebuttal argument.
- **Missing appeal deadlines**: Each plan has a strict appeal window (30–180 days from denial); missed deadlines forfeit the appeal right.
- **Altering documentation for appeal**: Backdating records, altering radiographs, or fabricating documentation to support a claim is insurance fraud.

---

## Evidence Notes

- **Appeal success rates**: Industry data suggests 30–50% of appealed dental claims are overturned; rates are higher with complete documentation and clinical narratives.
- **Peer review rights**: Most states grant patients and providers the right to request an external clinical peer review when coverage is denied for medical necessity reasons; this right is underutilized.
- **ADA CDT Companion**: The ADA publishes an annual CDT Companion Guide with guidance on documentation requirements for each code category — a useful reference for appeal letter writing.
- **Coordination of benefits (COB)**: When patients have dual coverage, COB rules determine primary and secondary payer order; COB disputes are a frequent source of denials requiring coordination with both plans.

## Summary

Periodontal maintenance therapy (PMT), coded as D4910, is the cornerstone of long-term periodontitis management following active periodontal therapy (SRP or surgery). The AAP defines PMT as a continuation of periodontal therapy to preserve clinical attachment levels, minimize disease recurrence, and monitor disease status. PMT is a distinct service from a prophylaxis (D1110) and must not be substituted for patients with a history of treated periodontitis.

The evidence base strongly supports that compliance with PMT reduces tooth loss, slows disease progression, and improves systemic health outcomes in periodontitis patients. Maintenance interval is typically 3–4 months for periodontitis patients, though this should be individualized based on disease grade, risk factors, and treatment response.

## Clinical Indications

### Indications for D4910 (Periodontal Maintenance)
- Any patient who has received active periodontal therapy (SRP or periodontal surgery) for a diagnosis of periodontitis.
- Patients with a documented history of periodontitis, even if currently stable.
- Patients with peri-implantitis who have completed active treatment.
- Patients with chronic conditions that predispose to recurrence: uncontrolled diabetes, smoking, immunosuppression.

### Components of a Periodontal Maintenance Visit
1. **Medical history review and update**: Identify new medications, systemic changes, tobacco status.
2. **Oral cancer screening**: Visual and palpation exam; adjunctive devices as indicated.
3. **Periodontal chart update**: Full 6-point probing at least annually; abbreviated probing at interim visits with documentation of any changed sites.
4. **Radiographic monitoring**: Vertical bitewings (or FMX) at appropriate intervals to monitor bone levels (typically 12–24 months for maintenance patients; shorter if clinical signs change).
5. **BOP assessment**: Full-mouth BOP%; trend over visits is more informative than single measurements.
6. **Calculus assessment and supragingival prophylaxis**: Remove supra- and accessible subgingival deposits.
7. **Site-specific subgingival debridement**: At sites showing active disease indicators (BOP, increased PD, suppuration); this is the key clinical distinction from prophylaxis.
8. **Oral hygiene reinforcement**: Review of home care technique; interdental cleaning assessment.
9. **Reassessment and treatment adjustment**: If multiple sites show disease reactivation, active SRP (D4341) may be re-indicated rather than maintenance alone.

### Recommended Maintenance Intervals by Risk Level
| Risk/Grade | Interval | Rationale |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Grade A (low) post-treatment stable | 6 months | Slow progression; low risk |
| Grade B (moderate) | 4 months | Moderate progression risk |
| Grade C (high risk: smoker, diabetic) | 3 months | Rapid progression risk |
| Recent active disease/surgery | 3 months | Post-op monitoring |
| Post-antibiotic adjunct therapy | 3 months for first year | Monitor response |

## Contraindications

- **D4910 for a patient who has never received active periodontal therapy**: D4910 requires prior active treatment documentation; first-time periodontitis patients need D4341 SRP, not maintenance.
- **Extending maintenance intervals beyond 6 months in Grade B–C patients**: Evidence shows disease reactivation increases when intervals exceed 6 months in moderate-high risk patients.
- **Substituting prophylaxis (D1110) for D4910 in periodontitis patients**: D1110 does not include the subgingival debridement component that defines maintenance therapy; it is appropriate only for gingivitis and healthy patients.

## Evidence Notes

- **PMT efficacy**: Lindhe & Nyman (1984) demonstrated in a landmark 14-year study that patients in compliance with 3-month maintenance had significantly less tooth loss (0.1 teeth/patient/year) compared to non-compliant patients (1.0–2.1 teeth/patient/year).
- **Compliance rates**: Studies consistently show that only 30–50% of periodontitis patients maintain compliance with recommended PMT schedules; practice-based recall systems improve rates.
- **3-month interval evidence**: Research on subgingival microbiota shows that pathogenic bacterial populations re-establish within 6–11 weeks after professional debridement; 3-month intervals disrupt this cycle in high-risk patients (Magnusson et al., 1984).
- **Systemic benefits**: Periodontitis treatment and maintenance are associated with improved glycemic control (0.3–0.5% HbA1c reduction), reduced CRP, and reduced cardiovascular risk in population studies.
- **Photodynamic therapy and laser adjuncts**: Emerging adjunctive treatments at maintenance visits; limited RCT evidence for additional benefit over conventional debridement; not yet standard of care.
- **Full-mouth disinfection**: Quirynen protocol (two-appointment full-mouth debridement within 24 hours with adjunct chlorhexidine) shows modest benefit in some studies but is not universally adopted.

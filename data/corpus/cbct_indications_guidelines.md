## Summary

Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) provides three-dimensional volumetric imaging of the maxillofacial complex at radiation doses lower than medical CT but substantially higher than conventional 2D intraoral radiographs. CBCT offers superior diagnostic capability for complex anatomy, spatial relationships, and pathology that cannot be adequately characterized by conventional radiographs.

CBCT use in dentistry has expanded rapidly; however, the AAOMR, AAE, AAP, AAID, and others emphasize that CBCT should be prescribed only when the anticipated diagnostic benefit exceeds the additional radiation risk — the ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) principle. CBCT is not a first-line screening tool.

## Clinical Indications

### AAOMR-Endorsed Indications by Specialty

**Implantology**
- Assessment of available bone volume (height, width, bone quality) prior to implant placement.
- Identification of proximity to inferior alveolar canal, maxillary sinus floor, adjacent tooth roots.
- Evaluation of sites with complex anatomy (narrow ridge, sinus pneumatization, angled placement).
- Post-operative evaluation of osseointegration complications.

**Endodontics**
- Detection of periapical pathology when conventional radiographs are equivocal.
- Assessment of complex root canal anatomy (extra canals, root curvature, C-shaped canals).
- Evaluation of root resorption (internal vs. external; extent and location).
- Pre-surgical planning for periradicular surgery (apicoectomy): root length, proximity to adjacent structures.
- Assessment of traumatic dental injuries affecting root or alveolar bone.
- Diagnosis of vertical root fractures when clinical findings are inconclusive.
- AAE position (2015): CBCT recommended when conventional radiographic assessment is insufficient and the diagnostic information would alter treatment decisions.

**Periodontics**
- Assessment of alveolar bone morphology, defect dimensions, and furcation involvement prior to regenerative surgery.
- Evaluation of bone grafting sites and post-surgical bone fill.
- Peri-implantitis bone loss characterization.
- AAP recommends CBCT when 2D radiographs are insufficient for surgical planning.

**Orthodontics**
- Impacted canine localization and root proximity assessment.
- Root resorption monitoring during orthodontic treatment.
- Skeletal assessment for orthognathic surgery planning (airway, cephalometric analysis).
- Supernumerary tooth localization.

**Oral Surgery / Pathology**
- Impacted third molar proximity to inferior alveolar nerve (Rood's signs on panoramic; CBCT when high risk).
- Characterization of jaw cysts and tumors (extent, cortical involvement).
- Temporomandibular joint assessment (condylar morphology, articular surface).
- Trauma: fracture extent, displacement.

**Pediatric / Growth Assessment**
- Cleft lip/palate bone graft assessment.
- Eruption pattern assessment for impacted/ectopic teeth.

### Volume Selection
- **Small FOV (≤5 cm)**: Single tooth/quadrant; lowest dose; indicated for endodontic and localized implant assessment.
- **Medium FOV (5–10 cm)**: Quadrant or arch; orthodontic/oral surgery planning.
- **Large FOV (>10 cm)**: Full maxillofacial; orthognathic surgery, pathology, trauma.
- **Principle**: Use the smallest FOV that captures the region of interest.

## Contraindications

- **Routine/screening CBCT**: Not indicated as a substitute for clinical examination or conventional radiographs in the absence of a specific diagnostic question.
- **Children without clear indication**: Growing patients are more radiosensitive; benefits must clearly outweigh dose.
- **Pregnancy**: Defer non-urgent CBCT if possible.
- **When conventional radiography is adequate**: If 2D imaging provides sufficient diagnostic information, CBCT is not indicated.
- **CBCT for caries detection**: CBCT has poor sensitivity for early caries and should not be used for caries diagnosis; bitewings remain the gold standard.

## Evidence Notes

- **Radiation dose**: CBCT dose ranges from 20–600 µSv depending on FOV and machine; a standard 4-BW series delivers ~6 µSv; a large FOV CBCT delivers up to 600 µSv. Annual background radiation is ~3,000 µSv.
- **Diagnostic yield**: A systematic review (Tyndall & Rathore, 2008) found CBCT changed endodontic diagnosis or treatment planning in 62% of complex cases when added to conventional radiographs.
- **VRF detection**: CBCT has sensitivity of ~79% and specificity of ~93% for VRF detection vs. ~28% sensitivity for conventional periapical radiographs (Hassan et al., 2009).
- **Incidental findings**: Large FOV CBCT frequently reveals incidental findings (maxillary sinus disease, calcifications, unrecognized pathology); practitioners must have protocols for systematic review and documentation.
- **AI and CBCT**: Deep learning algorithms for CBCT-based pathology detection (periapical lesion, caries, bone loss) are under active development; FDA-cleared tools for implant planning and caries detection are commercially available.

## Summary

Comprehensive periodontal charting is the foundation of periodontal diagnosis, treatment planning, and ongoing disease monitoring. It provides the objective data needed to assign an AAP 2017 Stage/Grade diagnosis, justify insurance coverage for periodontal services, and measure treatment response over time. Inadequate charting is one of the most common causes of insurance claim denials for scaling and root planing, and it creates significant medicolegal vulnerability.

The standard of care requires a full-mouth periodontal chart at new patient exams and at least annually for patients with a history of periodontal disease; patients on periodontal maintenance (D4910) should have a comprehensive periodontal evaluation at appropriate recall intervals.

---

## Clinical Indications

### Minimum Data Points for a Complete Periodontal Chart

**Probing Depths (PD)**
- Six measurements per tooth: mesiobuccal, buccal, distobuccal, mesiolingual, lingual, distolingual.
- Recorded in millimeters; measured from the free gingival margin to the base of the sulcus/pocket.
- Universal numbering system for teeth.

**Clinical Attachment Level (CAL)**
- Calculated as: PD + recession (when recession present) OR PD − hyperplasia (when pseudo-pocketing from gingival enlargement).
- CAL is the gold standard for assessing true attachment status; probing depth alone is insufficient.
- Required for AAP staging.

**Bleeding on Probing (BOP)**
- Recorded as present/absent for each site; BOP within 30 seconds of probing is positive.
- Full-mouth BOP% is a key inflammation index: >10% indicates active disease or poor control.
- BOP is a predictor of future attachment loss when consistently positive at the same site.

**Recession/Hyperplasia**
- Recession: distance from CEJ to free gingival margin (positive value).
- Hyperplasia (pseudo-pocket): gingival margin coronal to CEJ (negative value).
- Both affect CAL calculation.

**Furcation Involvement**
- Classified as Class I (probe enters <3 mm), Class II (probe enters ≥3 mm but does not exit), or Class III (probe exits through-and-through).
- Furcation class is a key Stage III/IV determinant.

**Mobility**
- Miller Mobility Index: Class I (slight movement <1 mm horizontal), Class II (>1 mm horizontal, no vertical), Class III (vertical mobility present).
- Grade 2+ mobility is a Stage IV criterion under 2017 classification.

**Tooth Status**
- Presence/absence; implant notation; endodontic treatment noted; restorations visible subgingivally.

**Suppuration**
- Presence of purulent exudate on probing; noted at affected sites.

### Recommended Charting Intervals
- **New patient comprehensive periodontal evaluation (D0180)**: Required at initial exam.
- **Periodontal maintenance patients (D4910)**: Comprehensive periodontal evaluation at least annually or as needed to reassess disease status.
- **Post-SRP re-evaluation**: 4–8 weeks after completing SRP; new chart to assess response and determine need for surgical referral.
- **Active disease monitoring**: Probing at each maintenance visit; full 6-point chart annually minimum.

---

## Contraindications

- **Single-point probing per tooth**: A single measurement per tooth is inadequate for documentation; six-point full-mouth charting is required.
- **Outdated charts for insurance claims**: Using a chart >36 months old for SRP claim submission typically results in denial; current radiographs and charting must accompany the claim.
- **Charting without clinical attachment level**: Insurance payers and medicolegal standards require CAL; probing depths alone are insufficient.

---

## Evidence Notes

- **BOP as disease predictor**: Lang et al. (1986) demonstrated that absence of BOP has a high negative predictive value for attachment loss (~98%); consistent BOP at the same site predicts future loss.
- **Reproducibility**: Inter-examiner variability of manual probing is ±1 mm; electronic probing devices (Florida Probe) reduce variability to ±0.5 mm but are not universally adopted.
- **Documentation and legal protection**: A comprehensive periodontal chart in the record is a primary defense in dental malpractice claims alleging missed periodontal disease.
- **EHR integration**: Modern EHR platforms (Eaglesoft, Dentrix, Open Dental) support automated periodontal charting with voice-entry, visual display, and color-coding for abnormal values.
- **AI-assisted charting**: Emerging AI tools can auto-detect periodontal parameters from probing data and radiographs, though accuracy for CAL derivation from radiography is variable.

## Summary

Pulp capping is a conservative, biologically-oriented technique to preserve pulp vitality when the pulp is either in close proximity to caries excavation (indirect pulp capping, IPC) or has been inadvertently or intentionally exposed (direct pulp capping, DPC). Both techniques aim to promote continued dentin formation and maintain a vital pulp, avoiding or delaying root canal therapy. Modern biocompatible materials — particularly mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) and calcium silicate-based cements (Biodentine) — have substantially improved success rates over traditional calcium hydroxide.

Pulp capping is gaining renewed clinical relevance as vital pulp therapy (VPT) expands the scope of biological pulp management, supported by evidence showing that even teeth with signs of reversible pulpitis can be successfully managed without endodontic treatment.

## Clinical Indications

### Indirect Pulp Capping (IPC)
Indicated when:
- Deep caries approaches the pulp but no frank exposure is present or expected.
- Remaining dentin thickness (RDT) is <0.5 mm as estimated clinically or radiographically.
- Pulp is vital with no signs of irreversible pulpitis (no spontaneous pain, negative percussion, no swelling).
- Modern approach: **selective caries removal** — leave caries-affected (leathery) dentin at the pulpal floor; place IPC material; restore. Single-step preferred over two-step (stepwise excavation) based on current evidence.

**IPC Materials:**
- MTA (ProRoot, Angelus): gold standard for reparative dentin bridge formation; biocompatible, hydrophilic.
- Biodentine (Septodont): calcium silicate cement; shorter setting time than MTA; supports IPC and temporary restoration.
- Calcium hydroxide: historical standard; acceptable but inferior to MTA in RCT evidence (higher failure, internal resorption risk).
- Resin-modified GIC (RMGIC): used as intermediate layer; not as first-choice capping material.

### Direct Pulp Capping (DPC)
Indicated when:
- **Mechanical/iatrogenic exposure**: Small pinpoint exposure (<1 mm) during caries excavation in a vital tooth with no preoperative symptoms.
- **Carious exposure in a vital tooth**: Increasingly supported by evidence when exposure site is ≤1 mm, hemorrhage is controlled, and tooth is symptom-free preoperatively.
- Pulp is vital with normal response to cold testing.
- No evidence of irreversible pulpitis (no spontaneous, lingering, or night pain).
- No radiographic periapical pathology.
- Adequate rubber dam isolation can be achieved for sterile placement.

**DPC Materials (preferred):**
- MTA: highest success rates in RCTs (Hilton et al., 2013); promotes tertiary dentin bridge.
- Biodentine: comparable outcomes to MTA in short-term studies; improved handling properties.
- Calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂): remains widely used; associated with tunnel defects in dentin bridge, higher long-term failure.

### Pulpotomy (Partial Pulp Removal)
- When exposure is >1 mm or contamination is present: removal of coronal pulp tissue to clinically uninflamed, hemorrhage-controlled level, then MTA/Biodentine placement.
- Full coronal pulpotomy increasingly used in permanent teeth as an alternative to RCT in teeth with irreversible pulpitis (Cvek pulpotomy for traumatic exposures; full pulpotomy for carious exposure in molars).

## Contraindications

- **Irreversible pulpitis**: Spontaneous pain, lingering thermal response (>30 seconds after cold stimulus removed), or night pain indicates the inflammatory process has extended beyond reversibility; root canal therapy is indicated.
- **Pulp necrosis or periapical pathology**: Non-vital teeth with radiolucency at apex require endodontic treatment.
- **Uncontrolled hemorrhage at exposure site**: Persistent, dark, or profuse bleeding suggests pulp inflammation beyond the exposure site; RCT or pulpotomy is indicated.
- **Large exposure (>2–3 mm)**: Success rates for DPC drop significantly with larger exposures; pulpotomy or RCT is preferred.
- **Poor prognosis tooth**: If restorability is questionable, pulp capping is not indicated.
- **Primary tooth with irreversible pulpitis**: Pulpotomy with formocresol or MTA, or pulpectomy/extraction — IPC/DPC not indicated.

## Evidence Notes

- **MTA vs. Ca(OH)₂**: Hilton et al. (2013, *JADA*) RCT found 80% success for MTA DPC vs. 59% for Ca(OH)₂ at 2 years. Subsequent meta-analyses confirm MTA's superiority.
- **Biodentine**: Rajasekharan et al. (2014) found no significant difference between Biodentine and MTA for DPC at 12 months; Biodentine advantages include lower cost and faster handling.
- **Carious exposure DPC**: A landmark study by Asgary et al. (2014) showed 97.9% success for CEM cement (MTA analog) DPC in permanent teeth with carious exposures and preoperative irreversible pulpitis symptoms — challenging traditional contraindications. Results require replication.
- **Vital pulp therapy trend**: Cochrane review (Ricucci et al., 2014) and IAENDO guidelines (2019) support expansion of VPT indications; full coronal pulpotomy in molars shows 90%+ success at 2 years in RCTs.
- **Dentin bridge quality**: MTA produces more complete, less tunnel-defective dentin bridges compared to Ca(OH)₂ in histological studies.

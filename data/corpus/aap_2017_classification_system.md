## Summary

The 2017 World Workshop on the Classification of Periodontal and Peri-Implant Diseases and Conditions, co-organized by the AAP and EFP, produced a comprehensive revision of the periodontal classification system previously established in 1999. The new classification reflects advances in understanding of disease pathobiology, incorporates staging and grading concepts from oncology, and adds categories for peri-implant diseases.

The 2017 system supersedes the 1999 classification and is now the standard for clinical documentation, research, and insurance coding. Its key innovation is the Stage/Grade matrix for periodontitis, which separately characterizes disease severity (Stage I–IV) from disease complexity and risk of progression (Grade A–C).

## Clinical Indications

### Classification Categories

**I. Periodontal Health, Gingival Diseases, and Conditions**
- Periodontal health on intact periodontium
- Periodontal health on reduced periodontium (successfully treated)
- Gingivitis — dental biofilm-induced (plaque-induced)
- Gingivitis — non-dental-biofilm-induced (e.g., viral, fungal, genetic, inflammatory, drug-influenced)

**II. Periodontitis**

*Staging* — based on severity and complexity of management:

| Stage | CAL | Radiographic Bone Loss | Tooth Loss (Due to Periodontitis) | Complexity |
|-------|-----|----------------------|----------------------------------|------------|
| I | 1–2 mm | Coronal 1/3 (<15%) | None | Max PD ≤4 mm, horizontal bone loss |
| II | 3–4 mm | Coronal 1/3 (15–33%) | None | Max PD ≤5 mm; up to Class I furcation |
| III | ≥5 mm | Extending to middle or apical 1/3 | <4 teeth | Vertical bone loss ≥3 mm; Class II/III furcation; max PD ≥6 mm |
| IV | ≥5 mm | Extending to middle or apical 1/3 | ≥4 teeth | Stage III + masticatory dysfunction, occlusal collapse, flaring/drifting |

*Grading* — based on rate of progression and risk factors:

| Grade | Rate of Progression | Primary Criteria | Risk Modifiers |
|-------|---------------------|-----------------|----------------|
| A — Slow | No evidence of progression | <2 mm bone loss over 5 years | Non-smoker; normoglycemic |
| B — Moderate | Evidence of progression | <1 mm bone loss/year | Smoker <10 cig/day; HbA1c <7% |
| C — Rapid | Rapid progression | ≥1 mm bone loss/year | Smoker ≥10 cig/day; HbA1c ≥7% |

**III. Other Periodontal Conditions**
- Necrotizing periodontal diseases (NUG, NUP, NUS)
- Periodontal abscess
- Periodontitis associated with endodontic lesions

**IV. Peri-Implant Diseases**
- Peri-implant health
- Peri-implant mucositis (reversible inflammation, analogous to gingivitis)
- Peri-implantitis (bone loss present; analogous to periodontitis)
- Peri-implant soft and hard tissue deficiencies

## Contraindications

- **Using the 1999 classification**: Practices must transition to 2017 classification for accurate diagnosis, treatment planning, and insurance communication; the old terminology (e.g., "slight/moderate/severe" chronic periodontitis) is deprecated.
- **Assigning Stage without CAL data**: Staging requires clinical attachment level (CAL) measurement; probing depth alone is insufficient because recession or hyperplasia can mask or exaggerate CAL.
- **Ignoring Grade in treatment planning**: A Stage II, Grade C patient (rapid progressor, smoker, diabetic) requires more aggressive treatment and shorter maintenance intervals than a Stage II, Grade A patient.

## Evidence Notes

- **Consensus process**: The 2017 classification resulted from a structured review of systematic reviews and consensus discussions by 90+ experts; the process and evidence base are published in the *Journal of Periodontology* and *Journal of Clinical Periodontology* (Tonetti et al., 2018; Caton et al., 2018).
- **Stage/Grade rationale**: Borrowing from oncology's TNM staging, the Stage/Grade matrix aims to predict treatment complexity (Stage) and disease trajectory/risk (Grade) separately, allowing individualized care planning.
- **Interexaminer reliability**: Studies post-implementation find moderate-to-good reliability for staging when examiners use standardized forms; grading is more variable due to requirement for longitudinal radiographic data.
- **Insurance coding alignment**: ICD-10-CM codes for periodontal diseases have been partially aligned with the 2017 classification; CDT codes are not changed by the classification but documentation should reflect 2017 staging terminology.
- **Implementation tools**: The AAP developed the Perio Risk Calculator and online staging tools to facilitate adoption; these are available at perio.org.

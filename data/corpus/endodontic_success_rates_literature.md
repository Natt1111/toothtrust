## Summary

Understanding the published success rates for root canal treatment (RCT) is essential for evidence-based treatment planning, patient counseling, and medicolegal documentation. Success in endodontics is defined across two main criteria: strict criteria (complete radiographic resolution of periapical lesion AND absence of symptoms) and loose/uncertain criteria (reduction in lesion size, minimal symptoms). Overall, initial RCT by an endodontist in a tooth without preoperative periapical pathology achieves 90–95%+ success; treatment of teeth with preoperative periapical lesions achieves 70–85% strict success.

Success rates vary by study methodology (cross-sectional vs. cohort), outcome criteria, tooth type, operator experience, and preoperative diagnosis.

## Clinical Indications

### Success Rate Summary by Clinical Scenario

| Scenario | Strict Success (Radiographic Healing) | Source |
|----------|--------------------------------------|--------|
| Initial RCT, vital pulp, no apical lesion | 92–97% | Ng et al. (2011); Salehrabi & Rotstein (2004) |
| Initial RCT, necrotic pulp, with periapical lesion | 74–86% | Ng et al. (2011) meta-analysis |
| Non-surgical retreatment of failed RCT | 75–83% | Torabinejad et al. (2009) |
| Periradicular surgery (apicoectomy) | 77–94% | Torabinejad et al. (2009) |
| Initial RCT overall (endodontist performed) | ~90% | Salehrabi & Rotstein (2004) — 1.46M teeth |
| Initial RCT overall (general dentist performed) | ~83–88% | Dummer et al. various |

### Key Prognostic Factors

**Factors associated with improved success:**
- No preoperative periapical lesion.
- Canal filled to within 0–2 mm of the radiographic apex.
- Absence of pre-existing periapical pathology.
- Vital pulp (necrotic pulp has lower success due to established infection).
- Adequate coronal seal post-treatment (critical for long-term outcome).
- Treatment performed by a specialist.
- Single-rooted teeth vs. multi-rooted.

**Factors associated with lower success:**
- Large preoperative periapical lesion (>5 mm).
- Canal length short of apex (>2 mm short).
- Intraoperative complications (perforation, separated instrument, ledge).
- Coronal leakage/delayed restoration placement.
- Periodontally compromised teeth.
- Tooth type: mandibular molars with complex anatomy show lower success rates than anterior teeth.

### Tooth-Type Survival (Long-Term)

Large administrative dataset studies provide "tooth retention" data (tooth still present) rather than strict radiographic success:
- **10-year tooth retention after RCT**: 96% in a 1.46M-tooth study (Salehrabi & Rotstein, 2004).
- This high survival rate reflects that radiographic success and tooth retention are different endpoints; many teeth with AP survive functionally even without complete radiographic healing.

## Contraindications

- **Over-reliance on population success rates**: Individual tooth prognosis depends on specific clinical factors; population averages should inform but not replace case-specific assessment.
- **Using success statistics to justify unnecessary treatment**: High success rates do not justify RCT on teeth with normal pulp/periapex without a valid clinical indication.

---

## Evidence Notes

- **Ng et al. (2011) meta-analysis** is the landmark systematic review of RCT outcomes: 63 studies analyzed; key finding that coronal seal is as important as technical quality of obturation.
- **Salehrabi & Rotstein (2004)**: Insurance database study of 1.46 million RCT-treated teeth; 97% tooth retention at 8 years, underscoring the long-term value of tooth retention over extraction.
- **Webber RT (1992)** and subsequent authors emphasize that "success" is poorly defined in the literature, complicating direct comparison across studies.
- **Endodontist vs. general dentist outcomes**: Studies consistently show 5–10% higher success rates for endodontist-performed RCT; complex anatomy (molar retreatment) gap is larger.
- **CBCT and success monitoring**: Annual bitewing or periapical radiographs remain the standard follow-up tool; CBCT is used for equivocal cases but not routine monitoring due to radiation.

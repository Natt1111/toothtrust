## Summary

The International Caries Detection and Assessment System (ICDAS) is a standardized, evidence-based method for detecting and classifying dental caries that has gained widespread adoption in clinical research and increasingly in practice settings. Unlike traditional binary classification (caries present/absent), ICDAS captures the full spectrum of caries severity from early demineralization through cavitation, enabling clinicians to make activity-appropriate treatment decisions — from remineralization for early lesions to operative intervention for cavitated ones.

ICDAS II (the current version) codes lesions on a 0–6 scale for coronal caries and incorporates a separate secondary code for restoration and sealant status. The system applies to all tooth surfaces and promotes a disease-management rather than drill-and-fill paradigm.

## Clinical Indications

### ICDAS Score Definitions and Treatment Thresholds

| Code | Description | Clinical Appearance | Typical Management |
|------|-------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| 0 | Sound | No evidence of caries | Preventive/monitoring |
| 1 | First visual change in enamel (seen after air drying) | White/brown spot only visible when dry | Remineralization: fluoride, dietary counseling |
| 2 | Distinct visual change in enamel (visible when wet) | White/brown spot visible on wet surface | Remineralization; consider sealing in pits/fissures |
| 3 | Localized enamel breakdown (no dentin visible) | Loss of enamel integrity; no dentin exposed | Sealing or minimal operative intervention |
| 4 | Underlying dark shadow from dentin (with or without enamel breakdown) | Shadowing through enamel from demineralized dentin | Operative; removal of caries and direct restoration |
| 5 | Distinct cavity with visible dentin | Dentin clearly visible in cavity | Operative restoration required |
| 6 | Extensive cavity with visible dentin (>1/2 of surface involved) | Extensive destruction; pulp proximity likely | Operative or endodontic evaluation; crown likely |

### Activity Assessment (ICDAS + ICCMS)
The ICDAS system is often paired with activity scoring (active vs. inactive lesion) from the International Caries Classification and Management System (ICCMS):
- **Active lesion**: Chalky white/yellow, opaque, rough surface texture; near gingival margin or in stagnation site.
- **Inactive lesion**: Brown/black, shiny, hard texture; often self-arrested.

Activity status directly influences whether to remineralize or restore: an active code-2 lesion warrants fluoride protocol; an inactive code-2 lesion may only require monitoring.

## Contraindications

- **ICDAS is not a stand-alone treatment decision tool**: Radiographic assessment must complement visual ICDAS scoring, particularly for proximal and sub-surface caries.
- **Code ≤2 does not always indicate "do nothing"**: In high-caries-risk patients, early lesions require active preventive protocols even if operative intervention is not yet indicated.
- **Not validated as a sole basis for insurance documentation**: CDT codes and insurance-required terminology differ from ICDAS; charting systems must translate appropriately.

## Evidence Notes

- **Reliability**: ICDAS has demonstrated high inter-examiner reliability (kappa 0.7–0.9) across multiple validation studies when examiners are trained. Untrained examiners show significantly lower reliability.
- **Sensitivity and specificity**: A systematic review (Ismail et al., 2007, *Community Dent Oral Epidemiol*) found ICDAS had high sensitivity for early caries detection compared to traditional methods, with acceptable specificity.
- **Clinical adoption**: While ICDAS is the research gold standard, practical adoption in private practice is limited; many practices use simplified 3-code systems. EHR systems increasingly support ICDAS scoring modules.
- **Radiographic correlation**: ICDAS codes 1–2 typically correspond to enamel-only lesions on radiographs; codes 4–5 typically show dentinal involvement on bitewings. CBCT offers superior detection but involves higher radiation dose.
- **Prevention focus**: The shift from Black's classification (I–VI cavity preparation categories) to ICDAS reflects the modern understanding that caries is a disease to be managed, not just a lesion to be drilled.

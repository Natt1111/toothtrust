## Summary

Pre-authorization (also called predetermination or pre-treatment estimate) is a process by which a dental practice submits proposed treatment to the patient's insurer before initiating care, to receive a written estimate of coverage and expected benefit payments. While pre-authorization is not always required, it is strongly advisable for high-cost procedures (crowns, bridges, implants, periodontal surgery) and is mandatory for some plans and certain procedures.

It is important to distinguish between:
- **Pre-authorization required (mandatory)**: Treatment without authorization may result in full claim denial.
- **Pre-authorization optional (voluntary predetermination)**: Treatment can proceed, but obtaining a predetermination helps estimate patient cost-sharing and reduces surprise denials.

## Clinical Indications

### Procedures Commonly Requiring or Benefiting from Pre-Authorization

**Typically Requiring Pre-Authorization:**
- Dental implants (D6010–D6067): Most plans require pre-auth; coverage varies widely.
- Orthodontic treatment (D8xxx): Coverage determination, lifetime maximum verification.
- Major restorative work: Some plans require pre-auth for crowns (D2xxx) or multi-unit bridges (D6xxx).
- Periodontal surgery (D4210, D4260, D4263): Pre-auth required by many plans before osseous or regenerative surgery.
- TMJ treatment (D9940–D9941): Often requires separate authorization and diagnostic documentation.

**Strongly Recommended (Voluntary Predetermination):**
- Crowns (D2740–D2752): Frequency limitations, downgrade provisions, and missing tooth exclusions are best identified pre-treatment.
- Fixed partial dentures/bridges (D6240–D6252): Missing tooth exclusion frequently applies; pre-auth identifies coverage before preparation.
- Endo + crown combination: Pre-auth clarifies whether plan covers both in the same benefit year.
- CBCT (D0364–D0368): Many plans require clinical indication documentation; pre-auth confirms coverage.

### Pre-Authorization Submission Requirements

**Standard Documentation Packet:**
1. Completed ADA Dental Claim Form (J400) with CDT codes.
2. Diagnostic-quality radiographs (current periapicals, bitewings, panoramic as applicable).
3. Periodontal chart for periodontal procedures.
4. Clinical narrative or letter of medical necessity for complex cases.
5. Photographs (intraoral) — increasingly requested for crown cases (showing fracture, decay, failed restoration).
6. Relevant medical history if cross-coding to medical insurance.

**Timing:**
- Pre-authorization takes 5–30 business days depending on plan and complexity.
- Submit at least 3–4 weeks before anticipated treatment date.
- Pre-authorization typically valid for 60–90 days (plan-specific); re-submit if treatment is delayed beyond the valid period.

### Pre-Authorization Workflow
1. Gather clinical documentation (radiographs, photos, chart, narrative).
2. Submit pre-auth to insurer (electronic via clearinghouse preferred; paper via mail as fallback).
3. Receive Explanation of Benefits (EOB) or predetermination letter with estimated coverage.
4. Review plan response: covered codes, downgrade provisions, exclusions, patient share.
5. Communicate to patient: insurance estimate, patient responsibility estimate, deductible/OOP status.
6. Obtain patient consent documenting financial responsibility beyond insurance.
7. Treat; submit final claim referencing the predetermination number.

## Contraindications

- **Treating before pre-auth is received when it is mandatory**: Results in automatic denial with limited appeal options; delay treatment until authorization is obtained or obtain patient's informed financial consent if treating before auth.
- **Relying on verbal pre-authorization**: Always obtain written (electronic EOB or letter) documentation; verbal authorizations are not binding.
- **Using pre-auth as a guarantee of payment**: Pre-authorization is an estimate based on current eligibility; actual payment depends on claim submission, frequency limits, remaining benefits, and benefit verification at time of claim.

## Evidence Notes

- **Predetermination utilization**: ADA Health Policy Institute surveys show that many practices skip predetermination for crowns, resulting in downgrade surprises (e.g., plan pays PFM rate for all-ceramic crown); routine predetermination reduces revenue loss.
- **Digital submission efficiency**: Electronic pre-auth submission via dental software (Eaglesoft, Dentrix, Carestream) integrated with clearinghouses (Emdeon/Change Healthcare, Availity) reduces turnaround time vs. paper.
- **Medical dental cross-coding**: Some procedures (dental implants for functional restoration, periodontal treatment for diabetic patients, oral surgery) may be covered under medical insurance using CPT codes; a predetermination to the medical plan requires CPT + ICD-10 coding, not CDT.
- **State mandates**: Some states require insurers to process predeterminations within specific timeframes; state insurance commissioner resources can be consulted if delays are excessive.
